Epic Universe Wait Times in Review: June 2026

July 1, 2026

A data-centric breakdown of when wait times peak, which days of the week have the lowest waits, and which rides are a risky bet during EPA, all from 49,580 wait-time readings.

Lines peak at 11 AM, then fall all day

The median standby wait climbs from about 10 minutes in the first hour to a 45-minute peak at 11 AM, holds there through midday, then eases back to about 20 by close. From 11 AM to 3 PM the median runs 40–45 minutes, roughly 1.6x the 25-minute median after 7 PM. (Averages sit higher, peaking near 53 minutes around noon, because a few long-wait headliners drag the mean up.)

The first hour and the last two hours generally have lower wait times (with a few exceptions).

June ran a touch busier than May overall: the park median edged up from 30 to 35 minutes.

Wait times barely vary by day of the week

Tuesdays and Saturdays have the lowest wait times, but day to day they barely move: the spread between the best and worst day is only about 4 minutes by average and 5 by median. That’s even flatter than May, when the spread was about 9 minutes by average and 10 by median.

Attraction uptime slipped a bit in a wetter month

Uptime is the share of time a ride was operating during park hours, with downtime split between weather and everything else. Rides that skip Early Park Admission aren’t counted as down during that hour. As one would expect, the indoor rides, like Monsters Unchained, Battle at the Ministry, and Mario Kart, didn’t log a single weather delay.

Mine-Cart Madness had a rougher month. It ran only 77% of its operating hours — down from 79% in May — the lowest uptime of any ride in the park, and its downtime was split almost evenly between weather and mechanical issues. Even with weather removed from the equation, it was still the least reliable attraction at Epic. Hiccup’s Wing Gliders logged the most closure events of anyone (109, at least one on all 30 days), though most were short blips averaging under half an hour.

Battle at the Ministry was the risky EPA bet this month

The EPA gamble flipped from May, when both headliners sat at roughly 1 in 4. In June, Battle at the Ministry climbed to a delay on 37% of EPA mornings, the worst odds in the park, while Mine-Cart Madness improved to about 1 in 6 (down from 26% in May).

Notes for June 2026

  • Any day is good to go to Epic Universe, with Tuesday and Saturday having slightly lower wait times.
  • Mine-Cart Madness is still the park’s most popular ride. It averaged a 99-minute standby, peaking at 120 minutes around 11 AM, and it’s hard to get on quickly at any time of day. The good news: if you have Early Park Admission, the EPA gamble eased this month, with the ride going down on only 17% of mornings (down from 26% in May). The bad news: its overall uptime dropped to 77% (from 79% in May), the worst in the park, so even midday it’s relatively unavailable.
  • Battle at the Ministry is the park’s other headliner, and in June it produced the single worst wait we tracked all month: 270 minutes on June 30. It averaged 95 minutes and peaked near 115 around 2 PM. It’s steadier than Mine-Cart on uptime (92%), but it carries the bigger EPA gamble now, with a 37% chance of a delay during early access (up from about 1 in 4 in May).
  • June was wet. Weather delays hit on 18 of 30 days, and the share of readings slowed by weather roughly tripled versus May (3.5%, up from 1.3%), with the storm window running roughly 1–7 PM. When the afternoon skies open up, pivot to the indoor rides that shrug off rain, such as Mario Kart or Monsters Unchained.
  • Arguably the best coaster in the park (and perhaps even all of Orlando), Stardust Racers, still averaged barely 20 minutes. That’s amazing! Weather did knock its uptime down to 89%, so check the app before you walk over.
  • Celestial Park is relatively more busy at the end of the day, with Constellation Carousel peaking around 8 PM, which contrasts with nearly all of the other attractions.
